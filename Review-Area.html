Finding the Area of Polygons

Finding the area of polygons depends on the specific shape and its given dimensions. Below is a structured breakdown of formulas, visual figures, and methods for finding the area of common two-dimensional polygons.

1. Standard Polygons

Polygon Variables Area Formula
Triangle \(b =\) base, \(h =\) height \(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} b h\)
Square \(s =\) side length \(\text{Area} = s^2\)
Rectangle \(l =\) length, \(w =\) width \(\text{Area} = l \times w\)
Parallelogram \(b =\) base, \(h =\) height \(\text{Area} = b \times h\)
Trapezoid \(a, b =\) parallel bases, \(h =\) height \(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} (a + b) h\)
Rhombus / Kite \(d_1, d_2 =\) diagonals \(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} d_1 d_2\)

2. Regular Polygons (Equal Sides & Angles)

For any regular polygon with \(n\) sides (such as a regular pentagon, hexagon, or octagon):

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} P a$$
  • \(P\) = Perimeter (\(n \times s\), where \(s\) is the side length)
  • \(a\) = Apothem (the perpendicular distance from the center to the midpoint of any side)

3. Advanced Methods for Irregular Polygons

Triangle via Side Lengths (Heron's Formula)

When you know all three sides (\(a, b, c\)) of a triangle but not its height:

$$\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}$$

$$\text{where } s = \frac{a + b + c}{2} \text{ (semi-perimeter)}$$

Coordinate Geometry (Shoelace Formula)

If the vertices of an \(n\)-sided polygon are given on a Cartesian plane as \((x_1, y_1), (x_2, y_2), \dots, (x_n, y_n)\):

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} |(x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_3 + \dots + x_n y_1) - (y_1 x_2 + y_2 x_3 + \dots + y_n x_1)|$$
