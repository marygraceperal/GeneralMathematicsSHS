1. Introduction to Financial Series

In real-world personal finance and business, transactions involving regular payments, investments, or loan repayments are structured using geometric sequences and series. Understanding how money grows or compounds over time allows individuals and businesses to make sound economic decisions.

Key Concept: Time Value of Money
A peso today is worth more than a peso in the future due to its potential earning capacity through interest. When regular deposits or loan payments are made over equal time intervals, we refer to this financial arrangement as an Annuity.

2. Future Value of an Ordinary Annuity (Investments & Savings)

The Future Value (\(F\)) of an ordinary annuity represents the total accumulated amount of a series of equal periodic payments (\(R\)) made at the end of each period, including compounding interest at a rate (\(i\)) per period over \(n\) total compounding periods.

\[ F = R \left[ \frac{(1 + i)^n - 1}{i} \right] \]

Where: \(F\) = Future Value (₱) | \(R\) = Deposit Amount (₱) | \(i = \frac{r}{m}\) | \(n = m \times t\)

Example 1: Accumulated Savings Account

Problem: Maria deposits ₱5,000 at the end of every month into a savings account that earns an annual interest rate of 6% compounded monthly. How much total money will she have in her savings account at the end of 3 years?

Step-by-step Solution:
  1. Given: \(R = \text{₱}5,000\), \(r = 0.06\), \(m = 12\), \(i = \frac{0.06}{12} = 0.005\), \(t = 3\), \(n = 12 \times 3 = 36\)
  2. Formula: \(F = 5000 \left[ \frac{(1 + 0.005)^{36} - 1}{0.005} \right]\)
  3. Calculation: \(F = 5000 \times 39.3361 = \mathbf{\text{₱}196,680.52}\)

3. Present Value of an Ordinary Annuity (Loans & Amortization)

The Present Value (\(P\)) of an ordinary annuity represents the current principal amount—such as the total loan value borrowed today—that can be fully paid off through a series of equal regular periodic payments (\(R\)) over \(n\) periods.

\[ P = R \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}{i} \right] \]

Example 2: Loan Amortization (Monthly Repayments)

Problem: Juan pays ₱12,000 at the end of every month for 2 years to repay a loan at 8% interest compounded monthly. What was the original principal amount borrowed (\(P\))?

Step-by-step Solution:
  1. Given: \(R = \text{₱}12,000\), \(r = 0.08\), \(m = 12\), \(i = \frac{0.08}{12} \approx 0.006667\), \(n = 24\)
  2. Formula: \(P = 12000 \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + 0.006667)^{-24}}{0.006667} \right]\)
  3. Calculation: \(P = 12000 \times 22.07607 = \mathbf{\text{₱}264,912.82}\)

4. Calculating Required Periodic Payment (\(R\))

When borrowing a fixed loan amount (\(P\)), we can compute the required regular payment (\(R\)) needed to pay off the debt within a designated timeframe.

\[ R = \frac{P \cdot i}{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}} \]

Example 3: Determining Required Quarterly Payment

Problem: An entrepreneur borrows ₱500,000 for a 5-year term at 9% interest compounded quarterly. What is the quarterly payment required?

Step-by-step Solution:
  1. Given: \(P = \text{₱}500,000\), \(i = \frac{0.09}{4} = 0.0225\), \(n = 4 \times 5 = 20\)
  2. Formula: \(R = \frac{500000 \times 0.0225}{1 - (1 + 0.0225)^{-20}}\)
  3. Calculation: \(R = \frac{11250}{0.35918} = \mathbf{\text{₱}31,321.34}\)

5. Summary of Formulas

Application Formula Purpose
Future Value (\(F\)) \(F = R \left[ \frac{(1 + i)^n - 1}{i} \right]\) Calculates total accumulated savings/investments over time.
Present Value (\(P\)) \(P = R \left[ \frac{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}{i} \right]\) Calculates initial loan amount supported by payments.
Periodic Payment (\(R\)) \(R = \frac{P \cdot i}{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}\) Determines payment needed to settle a loan balance.
