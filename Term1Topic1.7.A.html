Surface Area & Volume

Student Study Notes & Discussion Sheet

Key Terminology & Core Concepts

  • Surface Area (\(SA\)): The total area that the outer surface of a 3D object occupies. Measured in square units (e.g., \(\text{cm}^2\), \(\text{m}^2\)).
    • Lateral Surface Area (\(LSA\)): Area of all faces excluding top and bottom bases.
    • Total Surface Area (\(TSA\)): Sum of the lateral area and all base areas.
  • Volume (\(V\)): The total 3D space enclosed within a shape. Measured in cubic units (e.g., \(\text{cm}^3\), \(\text{m}^3\)).

3D Geometry Formulas & Exercises

1. Prisms

Polyhedron with two congruent, parallel polygonal bases connected by rectangular side faces.

  • \(B\): Base Area | \(P\): Base Perimeter | \(h\): Height
$$\text{Volume: } V = B \times h$$ $$\text{Total Surface Area: } TSA = 2B + Ph$$
Practice Exercises & Solutions:

Ex 1.1: A rectangular prism has a length of \(8\text{ cm}\), a width of \(5\text{ cm}\), and a height of \(10\text{ cm}\). Calculate its volume and total surface area.

Solution:
Base Area \(B = 8 \times 5 = 40\text{ cm}^2\)
Base Perimeter \(P = 2(8 + 5) = 26\text{ cm}\)
\(V = B \times h = 40 \times 10 = \mathbf{400\text{ cm}^3}\)
\(TSA = 2B + Ph = 2(40) + (26)(10) = 80 + 260 = \mathbf{340\text{ cm}^2}\)

Ex 1.2: A triangular prism has a right-triangular base with legs \(6\text{ m}\) and \(8\text{ m}\) (hypotenuse \(10\text{ m}\)) and height \(12\text{ m}\). Find its volume.

Solution:
Base Area \(B = \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 8 = 24\text{ m}^2\)
\(V = B \times h = 24 \times 12 = \mathbf{288\text{ m}^3}\)

Ex 1.3: Find the total surface area of the triangular prism in Ex 1.2.

Solution:
Base Perimeter \(P = 6 + 8 + 10 = 24\text{ m}\)
\(TSA = 2B + Ph = 2(24) + (24)(12) = 48 + 288 = \mathbf{336\text{ m}^2}\)

2. Pyramids

Polyhedron formed by connecting a polygonal base to a single apex point.

  • \(B\): Base Area | \(P\): Base Perimeter | \(h\): Height | \(l\): Slant Height
$$\text{Volume: } V = \frac{1}{3} B h$$ $$\text{Total Surface Area: } TSA = B + \frac{1}{2} P l$$
Practice Exercises & Solutions:

Ex 2.1: A square pyramid has a base side length of \(6\text{ cm}\) and a height of \(4\text{ cm}\). Calculate its volume.

Solution:
Base Area \(B = 6^2 = 36\text{ cm}^2\)
\(V = \frac{1}{3} B h = \frac{1}{3} (36)(4) = \mathbf{48\text{ cm}^3}\)

Ex 2.2: Find the slant height \(l\) and total surface area of the square pyramid in Ex 2.1.

Solution:
Slant height \(l = \sqrt{h^2 + (s/2)^2} = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5\text{ cm}\)
Perimeter \(P = 4 \times 6 = 24\text{ cm}\)
\(TSA = B + \frac{1}{2} Pl = 36 + \frac{1}{2}(24)(5) = 36 + 60 = \mathbf{96\text{ cm}^2}\)

Ex 2.3: A rectangular pyramid has base dimensions \(10\text{ m} \times 6\text{ m}\) and height \(9\text{ m}\). Calculate its volume.

Solution:
Base Area \(B = 10 \times 6 = 60\text{ m}^2\)
\(V = \frac{1}{3} B h = \frac{1}{3}(60)(9) = \mathbf{180\text{ m}^3}\)

3. Cylinders

3D solid with two parallel, congruent circular bases connected by a curved surface.

  • \(r\): Radius | \(h\): Height
$$\text{Volume: } V = \pi r^2 h$$ $$\text{Total Surface Area: } TSA = 2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2$$
Practice Exercises & Solutions:

Ex 3.1: A cylinder has a radius of \(3\text{ cm}\) and a height of \(7\text{ cm}\). Calculate its volume in terms of \(\pi\) and to 2 decimal places.

Solution:
\(V = \pi r^2 h = \pi (3)^2 (7) = 63\pi\text{ cm}^3 \approx \mathbf{197.92\text{ cm}^3}\)

Ex 3.2: Calculate the total surface area of the cylinder in Ex 3.1.

Solution:
\(TSA = 2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2 = 2\pi(3)(7) + 2\pi(3)^2 = 42\pi + 18\pi = 60\pi\text{ cm}^2 \approx \mathbf{188.50\text{ cm}^2}\)

Ex 3.3: A cylinder with radius \(5\text{ m}\) has a volume of \(300\pi\text{ m}^3\). Find its height.

Solution:
\(V = \pi r^2 h \Rightarrow 300\pi = \pi (5^2) h \Rightarrow 300 = 25h \Rightarrow h = \mathbf{12\text{ m}}\)

4. Cones

3D shape with a circular base that narrows smoothly to a single point called the apex.

  • \(r\): Radius | \(h\): Height | \(l\): Slant Height (\(\sqrt{r^2+h^2}\))
$$\text{Volume: } V = \frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h$$ $$\text{Total Surface Area: } TSA = \pi r l + \pi r^2$$
Practice Exercises & Solutions:

Ex 4.1: A cone has a radius of \(6\text{ cm}\) and a height of \(8\text{ cm}\). Calculate its volume.

Solution:
\(V = \frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3} \pi (6^2)(8) = 96\pi\text{ cm}^3 \approx \mathbf{301.59\text{ cm}^3}\)

Ex 4.2: Find the slant height \(l\) and total surface area for the cone in Ex 4.1.

Solution:
Slant height \(l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2} = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{100} = 10\text{ cm}\)
\(TSA = \pi r l + \pi r^2 = \pi(6)(10) + \pi(6)^2 = 60\pi + 36\pi = 96\pi\text{ cm}^2 \approx \mathbf{301.59\text{ cm}^2}\)

Ex 4.3: If a cone has a base area of \(25\pi\text{ cm}^2\) and a slant height of \(13\text{ cm}\), find its lateral surface area.

Solution:
Base Area \(= \pi r^2 = 25\pi \Rightarrow r = 5\text{ cm}\)
\(LSA = \pi r l = \pi (5)(13) = 65\pi\text{ cm}^2 \approx \mathbf{204.20\text{ cm}^2}\)

5. Spheres

Perfectly round 3D solid where every surface point is equidistant from its center.

  • \(r\): Radius
$$\text{Volume: } V = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3$$ $$\text{Total Surface Area: } TSA = 4 \pi r^2$$
Practice Exercises & Solutions:

Ex 5.1: A sphere has a radius of \(3\text{ cm}\). Calculate its volume.

Solution:
\(V = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 = \frac{4}{3} \pi (3^3) = \frac{4}{3}(27)\pi = 36\pi\text{ cm}^3 \approx \mathbf{113.10\text{ cm}^3}\)

Ex 5.2: Calculate the surface area of the sphere in Ex 5.1.

Solution:
\(TSA = 4\pi r^2 = 4\pi(3^2) = 36\pi\text{ cm}^2 \approx \mathbf{113.10\text{ cm}^2}\)

Ex 5.3: A hemisphere (half a sphere) has a radius of \(6\text{ m}\). Find its total surface area including its circular base.

Solution:
\(TSA_{\text{hemisphere}} = 2\pi r^2 + \pi r^2 = 3\pi r^2 = 3\pi (6^2) = 108\pi\text{ m}^2 \approx \mathbf{339.29\text{ m}^2}\)

Composite Solids (Combinations)

A composite solid is created by joining two or more 3D shapes together.

Calculating Composite Volume

  • Additive Solids: Calculate individual volumes and add them together (\(V_{\text{total}} = V_1 + V_2\)).
  • Hollowed Solids: Subtract the removed inner volume from the total outer volume (\(V_{\text{total}} = V_{\text{outer}} - V_{\text{inner}}\)).
⚠️ Important Surface Area Rule:
Do NOT simply add the total surface areas of combined shapes. Any internal connected faces are hidden and must be excluded from the total surface area calculation.

Discussion & Practice Problems

1. Comparison Challenge

A cylinder and a cone share the exact same radius \(r\) and height \(h\). What is the ratio of the cone's volume to the cylinder's volume? Explain why this relationship exists.

2. Capsule Problem (Hemispheres + Cylinder)

A medicine pill is shaped like a cylinder with a hemisphere attached to both ends. The overall length is \(14\text{ mm}\) and the cylindrical radius is \(3\text{ mm}\).

  • a. Find the total volume of the capsule.
  • b. Find the total exterior surface area.
3. Silo Problem (Cone + Cylinder)

A silo consists of a cylindrical body topped by a conical roof. Radius = \(4\text{ m}\), cylinder height = \(10\text{ m}\), cone height = \(3\text{ m}\).

  • a. Calculate the slant height of the roof.
  • b. Calculate the surface area that needs painting (excluding the base on the ground).

Answer Key & Detailed Solutions

1. Comparison Challenge

Answer: The ratio of the cone's volume to the cylinder's volume is 1 : 3 (or \(\frac{1}{3}\)).

Explanation:

  • Volume of a Cylinder: \(V_{\text{cylinder}} = \pi r^2 h\)
  • Volume of a Cone: \(V_{\text{cone}} = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h\)
  • Ratio: \(\frac{V_{\text{cone}}}{V_{\text{cylinder}}} = \frac{\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h}{\pi r^2 h} = \frac{1}{3}\)

This relationship exists because a cone tapers uniformly from a circular base to a point (apex), taking up exactly one-third of the total space contained within a cylinder of equal base area and height.

2. Capsule Problem (Hemispheres + Cylinder)

Given Parameters:

  • Radius (\(r\)) = \(3\text{ mm}\)
  • Total Length = \(14\text{ mm}\)
  • Height of Cylinder (\(h\)) = Total Length \(- 2(r) = 14 - 2(3) = 8\text{ mm}\)
  • Two hemispheres combine to form one full sphere of radius \(r = 3\text{ mm}\).

a. Total Volume (\(V_{\text{total}}\)):

$$V_{\text{sphere}} = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi (3)^3 = 36\pi\text{ mm}^3$$
$$V_{\text{cylinder}} = \pi r^2 h = \pi (3)^2 (8) = 72\pi\text{ mm}^3$$
$$V_{\text{total}} = 36\pi + 72\pi = \mathbf{108\pi\text{ mm}^3 \approx 339.29\text{ mm}^3}$$

b. Total Exterior Surface Area (\(SA_{\text{total}}\)):

Note: The interior circular bases where the cylinder joins the hemispheres are hidden and excluded.

$$SA_{\text{sphere}} = 4\pi r^2 = 4\pi (3)^2 = 36\pi\text{ mm}^2$$
$$LSA_{\text{cylinder}} = 2\pi r h = 2\pi (3)(8) = 48\pi\text{ mm}^2$$
$$SA_{\text{total}} = 36\pi + 48\pi = \mathbf{84\pi\text{ mm}^2 \approx 263.89\text{ mm}^2}$$

3. Silo Problem (Cone + Cylinder)

Given Parameters:

  • Radius (\(r\)) = \(4\text{ m}\)
  • Cylinder Height (\(h_{\text{cyl}}\)) = \(10\text{ m}\)
  • Cone Height (\(h_{\text{cone}}\)) = \(3\text{ m}\)

a. Slant Height of Roof (\(l\)):

$$l = \sqrt{r^2 + h_{\text{cone}}^2} = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{16 + 9} = \sqrt{25} = \mathbf{5\text{ m}}$$

b. Surface Area to Paint (excluding base on the ground):

Area consists of the Lateral Area of the conical roof + Lateral Area of the cylindrical body:

$$LSA_{\text{cone}} = \pi r l = \pi (4)(5) = 20\pi\text{ m}^2$$
$$LSA_{\text{cylinder}} = 2\pi r h_{\text{cyl}} = 2\pi (4)(10) = 80\pi\text{ m}^2$$
$$SA_{\text{paint}} = 20\pi + 80\pi = \mathbf{100\pi\text{ m}^2 \approx 314.16\text{ m}^2}$$
